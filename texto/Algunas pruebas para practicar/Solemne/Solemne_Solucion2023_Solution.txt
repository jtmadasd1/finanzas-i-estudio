Question II:  
2a) In the course, we were discussing three ways in which some investors can have more 
information than others. First, in an illegal way, some investors can obtain internal information 
from companies (through friends or relatives working within those companies). Second, some 
investors may have exceptionally powerful computers and highly skilled teams, including 
members with PhDs in Finance, Economics, Mathematics, Programming, amongst others. In this 
second scenario, these sophisticated teams can extract more knowledge from publicly available 
information. What is the third way in which certain agents can possess more information than 
others? Please provide an example. 
Answer: 
 
The third way in which some agents can possess more information than others is related to the 
learning process and the attention of agents. Naturally, learning new information (after market 
events that affect investments) takes time. In addition, investors sometimes are not fully focused 
on monitoring their investments because they have other tasks to attend to. Consequently, due 
to the time required for the learning process and the inattention of investors, some quicker 
investors can gain access to new information more rapidly and use it to their advantage against 
slower-learning and inattentive investors. 
 
For example, an investor with investments who is going for lunch may miss new information 
regarding such investments, and quick investors can exploit the inattentive investor's 
investment positions. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
2b) In class, we discussed the concept "walk up the book", in relation to some illegal practices of 
some managers and accountants, where they modify some numbers of the accounting book of 
firms. Please, provide an example of such illegal practices in companies. 
Answer 
This sentence is not true, since the concept of "walking up the book" is not related to the 
accounting book of firms (and it is not illegal). This concept is related to trading, when a large 
order is sent to be traded instantaneously in the market (e.g., a large market order in limit order 
markets), where this large order is traded at different prices. This occurs because there are not 
enough limit orders waiting at a single price to execute such a large order. Thus, this large order 
is "walking" at different prices in the limit order book.
